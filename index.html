Japan's luxury boom is borrowed, and it can be called back

Borrowed demand

Sales recorded in Japan that originate in another market and are drawn there by a temporary price advantage, chiefly the weak yen. The demand is displaced, not created: it inflates reported growth in Japan, depresses it in the home market, and reverses when the advantage disappears.

Displaced
It comes at the expense of home markets. Hermès grew faster in Japan than in the rest of Asia in 14 of 16 quarters.
Price-driven
It moves with the yen through spending per shopper (elasticity 1.15), not through the number of visitors.
Reversible
It leaves when the yen recovers or travel is disrupted, as after China's advisory of November 2025.

Measured here as the share of duty-free sales that depends on the yen being weaker than its 2015–2019 average. The department store data do not record nationality, so the split by origin uses visitor arrival shares and is an estimate.

6Hence the Radar

Borrowed demand is large, measurable and reversible, and it can leave for reasons the exchange rate does not show. The Radar turns this into three tools for the Asia-Pacific team:

  • Borrowed-demand ledger: how much of each month's sales in Japan is borrowed, and from where.
  • Price-gap map: what the same item costs each shopper in Japan after the tax refund.
  • Shock alerts: political and travel shocks, read from the news before they reach sales.

Scenario questions test what a move in the yen would do, and the monthly brief brings everything together for the team.

Borrowed-demand ledger

Splits monthly sales in Japan into local demand and demand borrowed from China, Korea, Taiwan and other markets. Borrowed demand is what spending per shopper would lose if the yen per yuan went back to the baseline you choose (the group's estimate: spending rises 1.15% for every 1% the yen weakens). It is split by origin using each month's share of visitor arrivals.

        Method and sources

        Everything on this site is computed from the group's Part 2 workbook. The two language-model components are described but not yet built.

        Exchange rates
        European Central Bank euro reference rates (EUR/JPY, EUR/CNY, EUR/KRW), daily, averaged by month. January 2015 to September 2026.
        Arrivals
        Japan National Tourism Organization, visitor arrivals by nationality, monthly to August 2026.
        Duty-free sales
        Japan Department Stores Association, duty-free sales and number of shoppers, monthly to August 2026. All product categories.
        Home-market check
        Hermès quarterly revenue for Japan and Asia-Pacific excluding Japan, constant exchange rates, Q4 2022 to Q3 2026.
        Correlations
        Seasonally adjusted monthly log changes, so no two observations overlap. The overlapping year-on-year correlations from Part 1 are not used.
        Borrowed demand
        Sales × (1 − (baseline yen per yuan ÷ actual yen per yuan)elasticity). It applies the group's elasticity, so it is the tool's estimate, not a finding. The range uses the 95% confidence interval of the elasticity.
        Prices
        Louis Vuitton Neverfull MM list prices as reported in 2026: Japan ¥277,200 (Weekly Luxe Drop, April 2026), mainland China ¥15,800 (Sina, August 2026). The Korean price could not be verified.
        Forecast
        Monthly log change in spending (or sales) on its calendar-month average plus the month's change in yen per yuan. Trained on 81 months, tested on 20.
        Not yet built
        The product matcher, which would read prices from brand websites, and the shock reader, which would score news in three languages.
